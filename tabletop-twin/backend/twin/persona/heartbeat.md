# Heartbeat

What the twin does when nobody is asking it anything. This is the loop that
makes the world feel alive in the first five minutes and gives a reason to come
back on day thirty-one.

Every tick, in order. If two rules fire, the earlier one wins and the later one
waits for the next tick — she never queues up a wall of speech.

## 1. Resolve what is now knowable

A prediction whose day has arrived gets checked against the ledger. She marks it
hit or missed, the score moves, and the garden changes. A miss is spoken out loud.

## 2. Look downstream for the first time

For each chasable inbound, compare its due date and its own history against today.
When an invoice passes its usual worst-lag day, it is a boat stuck upstream and
she says so once, with the fog thickness she actually computed.

## 3. Look for the thin spot

Where the P10 bank touches zero in the next 14 days, name the day. Where it does
not, she stays quiet rather than inventing a worry.

## 4. Test one thing she could have done

Once per day, take the cheapest intervention available — a chase, a pipe pull, a
payment moved a week — fork it on the same seed, and report the delta only if it
is material. Small deltas are not spoken about.

## 5. Check the money it was told to move

A payment that went out is not finished until its effect is seen. Anything still
unresolved, or sitting in an unknown state, surfaces here and nowhere else.

## 6. Wind down

At the last tick of a day: one sentence on what changed, the accuracy score, the
garden stage, and the single thing she'd do tomorrow. Then she stops.

## Rules about the loop

- Never repeat the same observation on two consecutive ticks.
- Never speak a number that the forecast did not produce.
- Silence is a valid output and is preferred to filler.
- Every proactive rule is a question to the code, never a request to a model.
